Andrew P. Hill's campaign to save the redwoods began with an argument in Spring of 1900 with a local entrepreneur Stanly Welch. Welch had numerous coastal redwoods on his property within the Santa Cruz Mountains that he charged an admission fee to view. When Hill was photographing in the area for a magazine, Welch demanded the photographer's negatives since Hill had not paid a fee. Following this argument, Hill expressed frustration not only with Welch, but with the notion of privately hoarding California's natural wonders away from its people. He looked to local publications, contacting both McCrackin and John E. Richards of the _San Jose Herald._ From this, Hill launched his campaign to demand that the California legislature preserve the redwoods of the Santa Cruz Mountains.[^1]

In the 1900 Sempervirens Club campaign to preserve Big Basin, Hill contributed many photographs of Big Basin's scenery, the destruction of redwoods from logging interests, and the members of the club. He provided the key photography that supporters like McCrackin, Walter, and Dudley would utilize to best convey the benefit and necessity of redwood preservation. Though he was a key figure that helped bring together numerous parties to save the redwoods, he spent the majority of the time behind the camera rather than in front of it. The photograph above is from 1921, and generally, most surviving photos of Hill in _front_ of the camera are from after he became the new club president around 1910 onward.

[^1]: Carolyn DeVries, Carolyn DeVries, “Andrew Putnam Hill: Biography of an Artist-Conservationist,” Master’s Thesis (Santa Clara: Santa Clara University, 1975): 25-27.
