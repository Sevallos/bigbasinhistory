title: 40,000 Signatures and Likeminded Clubs
---

In addition to the prominent community members the Sempervirens Club wrote to, the women of the club (and the San Jose Woman's Club) went door-to-door obtaining signatures to support their road construction bill. The club presented the signatures directly to the state legislature to prove to them that the people of California desired greater road accessibility to their state parks.[^1] This demonstrated the growth of the grassroots capability the Sempervirens Club had by 1912, and it was largely thanks to the partnership made between them and the San Jose Woman's Club back in May 1900.

Furthermore, California women's clubs had a great amount of networking capabilities among them. The women of the Sempervirens Club reached out to other Bay Area women's clubs through the California Federation of Women's Clubs. This federation had supported women's activism with environmental issues since 1900 following the campaign to protect the Calaveras Big Trees Grove. Since then, California club women linked together the wellbeing of their home with the wellbeing of their state's environment, as then CFWC President Clara Burdette called said environment:
> "The great natural reservoirs of the water supply that have made possible our homes, our health, and our prosperity."[^2]

Automobile enthusiast clubs also threw their support behind the Sempervirens Club. With automobile ownership on the rise, many enthusiasts desired more roads and more destinations to reach, such as the Good Roads Movement that William T. Jeter was part of.[^3]

[^1]: DeVries, 63.
[^2]: Cameron Binkley, “No Better Heritage Than Living Trees: Women's Clubs and Early Conservation in Humboldt County,” Western Historical Quarterly, Volume 33, Issue 2 (Summer 2002): 182.
[^3]: Binkley, 192.
