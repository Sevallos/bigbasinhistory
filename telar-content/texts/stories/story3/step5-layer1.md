---
title: Jennie Bliss Jeter and Her Husband William T. Jeter
---

Jennie Bliss Jeter first engaged with environmental activism as early as 1884, where she organized a "six-mile trek through the arduous terrain from Santa Cruz to Big Trees Grove."[^1] Her and her friends clad in Victorian-style dresses aimed to bring attention to widespread redwood deforestation taking place within the Santa Cruz Mountains. Though her 1884 campaign was not successful in halting deforestation, it led her to meet her future husband William T. Jeter, who would go on to become the Lieutenant Governor of California. The two of them continued to advocate for redwood preservation even before the Sempervirens Club, throwing their support fully into their cause once the May 1900 campaign kicked off.

As early as the 1890s, she helped lay the foundation for road accessibility into Big Basin when she proposed to the Santa Cruz Improvement Society the resurfacing and continued maintenance upon Big Trees Road, today's State Highway 9.[^2] This resurfaced road carried the Sempervirens Club into Big Basin for years via carriage when they rendezvoused in Boulder Creek, and later would link together with the automobile road that connected Big Basin with Saratoga. This along with her 1901 camping trip convey how important it was to her for California's beautiful parks to be accessible for families.


[^1]: Traci Bliss, _Big Basin Redwood Forest,_ 27.
[^2]: Traci Bliss, 29.
