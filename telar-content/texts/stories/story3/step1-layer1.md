title: Playgrounds for the Rich
---

![Old_Faithful_Inn_1914](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/952abae7-4eba-48be-ad7b-7c34ab405024){lg} _Old Faithful Inn, northest aspect._ Postcard from Yellowstone Photo Collection: Frank J. Haynes Collection, 1914.

In writing about the Sempervirens Club's visits to Big Basin and their campaign to preserve it, Josephine McCrackin often described Yosemite and Yellowstone National Parks as playgrounds for the wealthy.[^1] Her critique originated from the lack of accessibility to such spaces for the working class, as well as the parks' emphasis on wealthy accommodations such as luxury hotels. Furthermore, major railroad corporations held a monopoly over park accessibility for places such as Yellowstone and other midwest parks. Luxury train cars and luxury hotels priced many Americans out of visiting the recently protected parks. Critics like McCrackin asserted that if the parks were to be preserved for the American people, then they should be available to more than the wealthy elite.

[^1]: Josephine Clifford McCrackin, “How the Big Basin Redwoods Were Saved,” Out West, volume 20 (January, 1904): 33.
