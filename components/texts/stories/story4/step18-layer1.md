title: 2020 New York Times Article
---

Shortly after the 2020 fire at Big Basin began, _The New York Times_ published an article where the editors called on past visitors to the park to reflect on their experiences and memories. It resembled a time of mourning, as many of the people that wrote into the publication shared their cherished memories of visiting the park with loved ones. They talked about memories of family that had long passed, romantic nature hikes, childhood memories for both parents and children, and both academic and artistic inspiration that took place within the park.[^1]

As the 2020 fire raged on and even after it was finally contained, California State Parks grappled with how to reopen the park, how to make roads accessible to the public, how they will ensure the park's protection from environmental and humanmade dangers, and how they will spread word about the surviving redwood forests to welcome people back into Big Basin. 

These concerns and the article's many shared accounts mirrored the concerns and experiences of the Sempervirens Club a century prior. Big Basin had grown into a site of memory, of mourning, and of inspiration for visitors for over a century now. This article and its parallels to the prior history inspired me to research more about the Sempervirens Club and the period they came from. I grew very curious about the parallels and how California's oldest state park came to be.

[^1]: Jill Cowan, Sona Patel, and Shawn Hubler, “Memories of Big Basin Redwoods State Park: California Today,” _New York Times_, August 28, 2020, https://www.nytimes.com/2020/08/28/us/california-fires-big-basin-redwoods.html.
