I did not know of Big Basin before the fire or before I started my research interests a few years ago, but I made sure to visit the park upon its first couple months of finally reopening. The park was in a fragile state, closing and reopening every few days due to the weather that made road travel impossible. Many of its trees were scarred by the fire. Many of its campgrounds are still closed to this day, while the park center that had been constructed in the 2010s was burned entirely to the ground. Still, Big Basin was very lush and green when I visited with my mother and aunt. Even trees that were charred at the trunk had lots of life blooming at their treetops, and the sunlight able to reach the ground made many flowers bloom on the green grass. Big Basin suffered a disaster, but it did not get destroyed. The disaster still offered an opportunity for many of its visitors, docents, and administrators to reflect on how to rebuild and how to portray Big Basin's image in recovery to the public. My work was to help support the trends that give more recognition to many of Big Basin's early supporters, and from doing this work, Big Basin became a site of great memories as well.
