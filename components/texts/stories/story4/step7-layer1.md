---
title: Henry L. Middleton
---

Henry L. Middleton was a large stakeholder in at least one lumber milling company by 1900 when William T. Jeter met him. Initially, Jeter convinced Middleton of the potential benefits of bringing tourism to Boulder Creek through the potential preservation of Big Basin. Middleton was a businessman first and foremost. Sometime around 1900 to 1901, the Sempervirens Club convinced him to begrudgingly enter Big Basin alongside them. Though he was not initially sold on Big Basin's recreational value, by the end of the trek through the forest, he left Big Basin "inoculated with the spirit of this redwood-saving crowd."[^1] 

From that moment onward, Middleton supported the Sempervirens Club and their efforts for nearly two decades. He provided future club expeditions with dry goods from his store, camping equipment, and allowed his Commercial Hotel (pictured below) to be used as a rendezvous for club excursions into the redwood forest.[^2] As late as 1918, he was even involved with supporting the club's efforts to put on a forest play.
