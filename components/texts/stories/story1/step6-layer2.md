---
title: Refuge at Mitenne
---

When Spanish missionization efforts ramped up aggressively in the mid 1790s, Mitenne became something of a refuge for those resisting and fleeing the Spanish. Its distance from Santa Cruz, as well as its reputation for being "nestled in the hard-to-reach mountains," this Mitenne-bound fugutive community comprised not only of other Quiroste peoples but also fugitives from other neighboring tribes.[^1] The Quiroste's vast territory and influence over key exports may have made them an ideal nation to flee to for protection from the Spanish.

[^1]: Martin Rizzo-Martinez, _We Are Not Animals_, 59.
