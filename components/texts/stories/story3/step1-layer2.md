The Wawona Hotel was constructed in the late 1870s in Victorian-style architecture that offered wealthy guests luxury accommodations in the midst of scenic nature. To reach the hotel, guests would ride in a carriage from the nearest railroad station, allowing their entire trip to be as comfortable and accommodating as possible. As seen in Yosemite, Yellowstone, and Glacier National Parks, the lucrative tourism industry constructed guest expectations for such luxury. These luxuries became synonymous with the scenic nature, resulting in the reputation that McCrackin critiqued: the national parks were only available to the wealthy.
