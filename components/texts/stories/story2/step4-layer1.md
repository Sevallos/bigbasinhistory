---
title: "The San Jose Woman's Club"
---

In January of 1900, the California Federation of Women's Clubs (CFWC) was formed to establish a network of club women activism. Its first president Clara Burdette asserted that "home must always be the center, but not the limit, of woman's life." [^1]. This assertion extended the role of club women's moral activism beyond the home and local community and into California's rich environment. Burdette called for club women to engage in preservation activism to protect them from deforestation by "men whose souls are gangsaws... [who would cut] our world famous Sequoias into planks and fencing worth so many dollars." [^2]. Within months, the CFWC had its first major preservation success with their Calaveras Grove campaign, legitimizing the role of club women in environmental activism and setting the stage for the Sempervirens Club.

The San Jose Woman's Club (SJWC) attended this first CFWC meeting and internalized President Burdette's call to action. By May of 1900, they joined together with the Sempervirens Club to "Save the Redwoods" under the leadership of SJWC President Louise C. Jones (2nd from the left) and Carrie Stevens Walter (3rd from the left). Jones rallied the SJWC's 80 members to conduct grassroots activism while Walter served as the Sempervirens Club's secretary and addressed the California public through print media.

[^1]: Cameron Binkley, “No Better Heritage Than Living Trees: Women's Clubs and Early Conservation in Humboldt 
County,” _Western Historical Quarterly_, Volume 33, Issue 2 (Summer 2002): 182
