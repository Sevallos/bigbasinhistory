---
title: Mounting Pressure to Preserve Big Basin
---

Many written and spoken accounts leading up to the passing of the bill were critical to gaining support and to presuading the state legislature. Even after the bill's passage, they continued to advocate for the continued preservation, management, and opening up of the park:

> “...to preserve [Big Basin] not only for ourselves and our friends, but for the State, for the world, for future ages."[^1] 
**Louise C. Jones of the San Jose Woman's Club**

> "Soon a railway will be laid into and through this unique ideal region. That railway will be built, temporarily, to carry out from the Big Basin the noble redwood trees, whose last-abiding place is there; the bark of the splendid oak, that have no equal in beauty in the world… and then its worn-out tracks will be left to rot and burn with the other unsightly, worthless debris."[^2] 
**Frederick Clarke, Reporter and Photographer for Arthur A Taylor's _Santa Cruz Surf_**

> "We have the right to demand our Government that these trees, the Sequoia sempervirens… should be kept from utter extinction, should be preserved for future generations."[^3] 
**Josephine Clifford McCrackin writing for the _Overland Monthly_**

> “A vast cathedral by the western sea,
> Whose spires God set in majesty on high,
> Peak after peak of forests to the sky,
> Blended in one vast roof of greenery.
> The nave, a river broadening to the sea;
> The aisles, deep canyons of eternal build;
> The transepts, valleys with God's splendor filled;
> The shrines, white waterfalls in leaf-laced drapery.”[^4] 
**Lillian H. Shuey, local Bay Area poet**

> "This forest is an aggregation of arboreal wonders. It is moreover a cathedral, a university, a sanatorium, a source of solace to the soul, an inspiration to the intellect, a tonic to the body."[^5] 
**Arthur A. Taylor, owner of the _Santa Cruz Surf_**

> "...shows the people of this peninsula that the price of its most attractive natural feature is eternal vigilance, and not simply the $250,000 that was paid over from their state treasury."[^6] 
**Professor William R. Dudley of Stanford University**

[^3]: Josephine McCrackin, “About the Big Basin,” Overland Monthly, vol 36 (1900): 137.
[^4]: Lillian H. Shuey, Among the Redwoods (San Francisco: The Whitaker & Ray Company, 1901) 9.
[^5]: Arthur A. Taylor, _California Redwood Park, Sometimes Called Sempervirens Park; An Appreciation_ (Sacramento: W.M. Richardson, 1912) Foreword.
[^6]: DeVries, "Andrew Putnam Hill," 54.
