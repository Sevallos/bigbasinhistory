---
title: Save the Redwoods! Reaches the General Public
---

With photographs provided by Andrew P. Hill, Carrie Stevens Walter wrote a full-page article in the May 27th, 1900 edition of the  _San Francisco Chronicle_ newspaper. The article recounted the first expedition and the founding of the Sempervirens Club in early May 1900, both describing the area's natural beauty and the ways in which it was endangered by corporate interests. She utilized the full page to convey the sheer impressive size of coastal redwoods to her audience, while also highlighting the dangers present in photos like "Beginning the Destruction." The end of the article emphasized the urgency for preserving Big Basin, stating that "Once gone, no human power or ingenuity can replace them." Around the same time that the newspaper published this article, other Sempervirens and women's club members worked to lobby local politicians and university administration/professors to support their preservation cause.

Walter had a long career in education and writing. Up until the early 1890s, she worked as a school teacher before dedicating her work full-time to writing and publishing. She wrote numerous articles, short stories, and even poems that became well-known throughout Santa Clara County.[^1] Once the Sempervirens Club formed in May 1900, with the pledged support of the San Jose Woman's Club, Walter officially served as the Sempervirens Club's secretary. She remained a critical supporter of the club's goals up until her passing in 1907.
