Father Kenna, among the older supporters of the Sempervirens Club, served as a mentor figure for the club and especially the club's future president Andrew P. Hill. He was among the several supporters to speak before the state state, addressing himself "as a forty-niner" in reference to when he and his family first arrived in California. Even after the park bill passed, he continued to advocate for the park's continued expansion and especially its expansion of accessibility to the general public. His vision for the park greatly influenced how Hill would lead the Sempervirens Club in the 1910s, as he believed that the park should be "a breathing place for the people." While other national parks like Yellowstone and Yosemite had developed a reputation for prioritizing/accommodating wealthy visitors, Father Kenna believed that Big Basin should be accessible to the working class.

> "To save the trees for posterity… to form a great park for the people… whither our children and workmen factory girls and others breathing all week impure air, might amidst the great trees and along rippling Brooks breathe pure air, where their minds and hearts are lifted to hire pure nobler things."[^1]
